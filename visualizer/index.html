Bench testsPick one on the left

Connect the Arduino first (top right). The strip above, the charts and the serial log stay live whichever test is open. Each test keeps its last run in this browser and downloads as CSV.

  1. System check before each session: every part, nothing rewired.
  2. Calibrate once after any change to the servos, linkage or sensor mount.
  3. Free-hand checks the loop pushes the right way before anything spins.
  4. Laser (CT-1) proves the gimbal goes where it is told, within 1°.
  5. Throttle (CT-4) runs the EDF on its own and shows the vibration it adds.
  6. Thrust vector (CT-5) runs both: the thrust it takes to move the rocket off a rig stop.
  7. Stability (IT-3), motor off: how the rig holds the rocket. CG vs the pivot, pivot friction, and the collar slide and nose weight that balance it.

          Lift-off: the throttle where it came 2.5° off the stop (median of the repeats, ± half the spread). Starts moving: where it really began to move, back-dated from lift-off by a curve fit (it needs a moment to accelerate, while the throttle keeps rising). The thrust, side force and torque use this one. Thrust comes from your Throttle step test if you entered scale readings, otherwise from the spec curve. Side force = thrust × sin(gimbal). Torque = side force × 190 mm (pivot to gimbal). The torque should come out about the same at every gimbal angle: it is what the imbalance and pivot friction hold the rocket down with. Divided by the 598 g mass it gives how far the CG sits off the pivot.

                How it's worked out

                Each swing is fitted with accel = −k·(tilt − settle) − c·rate − f·sign(rate). CG: height of the CG above the pivot (− = below) from the gravity spring k: h = −k·I / (m·g). Below the pivot it swings back like a pendulum; above, it falls away; on it, it stays where it is left. Period: one swing there and back (fit; the release table also gives it from the peaks). Pull 5°: how hard gravity turns it back from 5°, which the TVC has to beat. Damping ζ: smooth slowing (air, grease). Friction: dry pivot friction torque. It stops the rocket anywhere within the dead band (friction ÷ k) and can make a tight axis look neutral when it isn't. Settles: where it came to rest. The two rig pivots cross at one point, so X and Y should give the same CG. Fix: loosen the knob and slide the rocket through the collar (up moves the pivot toward the tail, so the CG rises relative to it; the +x post scale reads 200–270 mm from the tail), then nose ballast for the rest, to reach the target.
