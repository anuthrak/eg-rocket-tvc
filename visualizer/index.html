Bench testsPick one on the left

Connect the Arduino first (top right). The strip above, the charts and the serial log stay live whichever test is open. Each test keeps its last run in this browser and downloads as CSV.

  1. System check before each session: every part, nothing rewired.
  2. Calibrate once after any change to the servos, linkage or sensor mount.
  3. Free-hand checks the loop pushes the right way before anything spins.
  4. Laser (CT-1) proves the gimbal goes where it is told, within 1°.
  5. Throttle (CT-4) runs the EDF on its own and shows the vibration it adds.
  6. Thrust vector (CT-5) runs both: how hard the gimbal can turn the rocket, at each throttle.

          Thrust vectorCT-5

          Finds how much thrust it takes to move the rocket. Start with it resting where it falls on the rig, leaning on a stop. The page points the gimbal so thrust pushes it back toward upright, then raises the throttle slowly while the Nano's IMU watches. The moment it comes 1° off the stop, the motor is cut and it settles back. That throttle is the lift-off point. Each repeat starts from rest again.

          Lift-off: the throttle where it came 1° off the stop (median of the repeats, ± half the spread). Starts moving: where it really began to move, back-dated from lift-off by a curve fit (it needs a moment to accelerate, while the throttle keeps rising). The thrust, side force and torque use this one. Thrust comes from your Throttle step test if you entered scale readings, otherwise from the spec curve. Side force = thrust × sin(gimbal). Torque = side force × 190 mm (pivot to gimbal). The torque should come out about the same at every gimbal angle: it is what the imbalance and pivot friction hold the rocket down with. Divided by the 598 g mass it gives how far the CG sits off the pivot.
